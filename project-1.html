AI-Powered BPM Development Factory

Problem

The organization's BPM systems were built on legacy Lotus Notes workflows — agent scripts, DXL forms, and flow exports — that were poorly documented and difficult for new development teams to understand. Rebuilding or extending these workflows required significant manual effort to reverse-engineer business logic before any development could begin, slowing down delivery and increasing the risk of missed requirements.

Solution

I designed an AI-powered development framework that reshaped how BPM delivery works end to end. The framework starts by reverse-engineering legacy Lotus Notes artifacts — agent scripts, DXL forms, and flow exports — into clear, structured functional specifications. These specifications then feed into an AI-assisted development pipeline that accelerates coding, supports structured testing, and streamlines resolution of issues raised during User Acceptance Testing (UAT). The result is a repeatable "development factory" model that shortens the path from legacy analysis to production release.

Tech Stack

  • Generative AI (GenAI) tooling for code and specification generation
  • Lotus Notes / HCL Domino (agent scripts, DXL, workflow exports)
  • Structured specification and documentation frameworks
  • BPM platform development & testing tools

Your Role

I led the design of the AI-powered development framework as Project Manager, defining the reverse-engineering methodology and coordinating between business analysts, developers, and QA teams. I was responsible for establishing the AI-assisted workflow, driving UAT issue triage and resolution, and ensuring releases met both business requirements and governance standards.

Impact / Metrics

  • Significantly reduced time spent reverse-engineering legacy BPM workflows before development
  • Established a repeatable AI-assisted delivery process adopted across BPM development cycles
  • Improved traceability between legacy business logic and new system specifications
