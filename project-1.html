AI-Powered BPM Development Factory

Business Context & Problem

The company had long relied on an aging Lotus Notes system to handle form submissions and approval workflows across HR, administration, and IT — more than sixteen commonly used forms in total. As the Notes platform grew harder to maintain and extend, and could no longer meet modern integration needs (such as connecting to ERP and EHR systems), the company launched a replacement initiative to retire the legacy system in favor of a self-built platform, UniCore BPM 2.0.

The core problem broke down into three layers:

  • System layer: The Notes architecture was aging and hard to maintain, making it costly to add or modify forms and workflows.
  • Process layer: Approval logic and routing rules had accumulated for years inside Notes code and flow designs, with no unified standard, making them difficult to audit or extend.
  • Integration layer: The legacy system couldn't effectively connect with other enterprise systems (ERP, EHR, etc.), creating an information silo.

The goal of this project was to build an enterprise-grade workflow platform on FastAPI and Vue 3, with a generic form engine and a multi-step approval workflow engine, and to migrate 16+ forms and workflows from the legacy system to the new platform safely and in a controlled way.

My Role & Key Challenges

My role: Project Manager / AI Transformation Lead. I owned requirements clarification, migration scheduling, and cross-department communication for the overall project, and led the adoption of AI-assisted development into the existing engineering workflow as the core lever for accelerating both legacy reverse-engineering and new-platform development.

Stakeholders

  • IT department: operations and integration point of contact, ensuring the new platform met existing infrastructure standards
  • Senior leadership: budget approval and direction, focused on migration risk and go-live timeline

Constraints

  • Legacy baggage: had to correctly carry over existing Notes form data and process design without disrupting the business
  • Limited resources: 16+ forms had to be migrated incrementally, making AI assistance essential to throughput

Risks

  • Correctness of AI-assisted output: unverified conversions could mistranslate implicit logic buried in Notes
  • User adoption: the new platform needed to be intuitive enough, backed by communication and training, to lower resistance

Approach & Solution

The strategy combined three elements: a modernized architecture, generic engines, and AI-assisted migration.

  • FastAPI + Vue 3 modern architecture: A high-performance FastAPI backend and a Vue 3 frontend for the admin console and user portal, replacing Notes' closed client architecture with far better maintainability and extensibility.
  • Schema-driven form engine: Form fields, validation rules, and display logic are defined via schema instead of hard-coded per form, so creating new forms or adjusting existing ones becomes a configuration task rather than a development one — substantially cutting development cost.
  • BPM 2.0 multi-step approval engine: Supports conditional routing, parallel approvals, authority rules, and organizational hierarchy — replacing the fragmented, hard-to-audit process logic scattered across Notes with one controllable, traceable workflow core shared by every form.
  • AI-assisted reverse engineering (Notes conversion toolchain): For legacy assets (exported DXL form designs, exported flow definitions, field script logic), I built a set of AI-assisted conversion tools that automatically translate Notes design assets into draft schemas, workflow step configurations, and field logic (visible_when / required_when / auto_fill / compute) for the new platform — reviewed and confirmed by a human before publishing, dramatically accelerating what used to be heavily manual comparison work.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

Decision

Use AI to generate, but keep human verification at every critical step.

Facing the tension between "using AI to speed up migration" and "guaranteeing correctness in a production system," I chose not to pursue full automation. Instead, AI was positioned as an accelerator rather than the final decision-maker: it handled the highly repetitive, high-volume analysis and conversion work (form field comparison, workflow step translation), while form publishing and workflow go-live remained gated by human review and sign-off.

Why

When an enterprise approval workflow breaks, it affects real signing authority and compliance — the tolerance for error is extremely low. Compared with the speed advantage of end-to-end automation, keeping a human checkpoint better guarantees reliability in production.

What we gave up

Compared with a fully automated pipeline, this approach sacrificed some of the theoretical speed ceiling — migration still required human effort for review and validation, and couldn't be fully hands-off. In exchange, it delivered controlled risk and trustworthy output, which was the more pragmatic choice given limited resources and no room for repeated rework.

Deliverables & Impact

Deliverables

  • Approval workflow platform (BPM 2.0): a complete enterprise workflow system with multi-step approval, conditional routing, parallel sign-off, authority rules, a user portal, and an admin console.
  • Form conversion / reverse-engineering toolchain: automated tooling that translates Notes form designs, flow exports, and field script logic into assets usable on the new platform.
  • Standardized spec templates / migration process: a repeatable standard operating procedure and spec document template for each form migration, so work can be replicated, tracked, and handed off.

Impact

  • Reduced manual analysis and migration effort: AI-assisted reverse engineering significantly cut the time previously spent manually comparing legacy Notes forms and process logic
  • Improved development consistency and maintainability: the schema-driven form engine and unified workflow engine gave every form a consistent standard to build and maintain against, lowering the cost of future extension and debugging
  • Established a reusable AI-assisted delivery framework: this "AI-assisted analysis plus human verification" method and toolchain is replicable and can be applied to future system migrations or development projects

Lessons Learned

The real challenge of AI transformation isn't technical — it's about workflow redesign and adoption. The biggest bottleneck in adopting AI-assisted development usually isn't whether AI can produce correct code or conversions; it's whether the team redesigns its workflow to use AI well — where to introduce it, which checkpoints must stay human-gated, and how to build the team's trust in this new way of working. Technical capability is just the starting point; what actually determines a project's success is the process and organizational adaptability built around that technology.
