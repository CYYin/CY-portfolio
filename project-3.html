SRM–ERP Order Automation & Sync System

Role: Project Manager  |  Systems: SRM (MSSQL) ↔ ERP (Oracle)  |  Collaboration: cross-department, cross-border team

Business Context & Problem

Order data had long relied on staff manually re-keying the same information into both the SRM and ERP systems. The business team's own feedback was direct: entering orders one by one by hand was slow and error-prone, and it was the single biggest efficiency bottleneck in the process — which is what triggered this project in the first place.

The real-world impact of the problem:

  • Delivery delays — manual processing couldn't keep pace with order volume
  • High labor cost — significant staff time spent processing orders one at a time
  • Error-prone, missed orders — an inherent risk of manual data entry
  • Overtime for the business team — peak order volume was hard to absorb manually

My Role & Key Challenges

My role: Project Manager, owning requirements clarification, cross-team coordination, and go-live — the key link between business needs and technical implementation.

Stakeholders

  • Business department
  • IT department
  • The client's SRM system point of contact
  • Philippines-based development team

Constraints

  • Timeline pressure

Risks

  • The biggest challenge wasn't the technology itself, but the timeline pressure combined with cross-company, cross-team communication — needing to align business expectations, IT's system standards, the client's SRM data rules, and the working rhythm of the Philippines development team, all at once

Approach & Solution

The solution was to build an automated order sync system that replaced manual entry with system integration, letting order data flow reliably between SRM and ERP on its own.

Architecture: SRM (MSSQL) → sync engine (a FastAPI middleware layer) → ERP (Oracle). The middleware runs both a scheduled comparison mechanism and a real-time event sync mechanism, and exposes a front-end console for staff to review exceptions.

Tech stack: FastAPI / Python, dual Oracle + MSSQL database integration, a React / Vite frontend, and a scheduler.

This project did not use AI-assisted development — the architecture and code were designed and built entirely by the team.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

Decision

Implement both the scheduled comparison mechanism and the real-time event sync mechanism together, rather than choosing just one, to balance data freshness with completeness.

Cost

Running both mechanisms in parallel noticeably extended the development timeline — but even with timeline pressure as the biggest constraint, reliability wasn't something we were willing to trade away.

Deliverables & Impact

Deliverables

  • The SRM–ERP automated order sync system — the core system replacing manual, order-by-order data entry
  • A front-end admin console — for business/IT staff to check sync status and handle exception records
  • A cross-department, cross-border collaboration process — the working model established with the Philippines development team and the client's SRM point of contact

Real-world impact

  • Substantially reduced order entry time — automated sync replaced manual, order-by-order entry, freeing up significant repetitive work for the business team
  • Eliminated overtime for the business team — peak order volume no longer depended on stacking up manual hours
  • Fewer human errors and missed orders — removing the manual entry step reduced both error and omission risk

Lessons Learned

Cross-border, cross-department communication is harder than the technical implementation itself. Working with the Philippines development team and the client's SRM point of contact, the real challenge was aligning understanding and expectations, not writing code — communication overhead is often the real variable that determines a project's timeline. Prioritization under time pressure is a decisive skill: with limited time and resources, judging which mechanisms had to be in place first and which could be refined later mattered more for on-time, stable delivery than chasing technical perfection.
